Built for you: loss-run-gym

Your problem #7 is that insurance has no ImageNet, and that synthetic data has to carry the right distribution of imperfection. So I built a generator for synthetic loss runs that reproduces the failures from your post: a policy number printed only in a section header, subtotal rows laid out like claims, blank cells meaning "same as above", details and financials split across tables, rows repeated at page breaks, a carrier-prefix typo in a claim number, and zero-value claims.

Every PDF comes with exact ground truth. The scorer pairs claims by normalised number, then by optimal assignment over coverage, date, claimant and description, and reports row count next to totals, since totals alone can look right while claims are missing.

A deliberately naive one-pass extractor on it

Clean files: row accuracy 1.000, field accuracy 1.000, 8 of 8 pass.
All traps on: row accuracy 0.971, field accuracy 0.433, 0 of 8 pass.

The row count looks almost right while most fields are wrong. That is the reason to read row count, field accuracy and totals together.

What I would do in my first two weeks

  1. Days 1 to 3. Run your extraction evals end to end and learn where accuracy is lost today. Add trap files for the failure modes you already know into the eval set, so a regression on any of them shows up before customers see it.
  2. Days 4 to 8. Work on consolidating underwriter corrections: group corrections that say the same thing, flag ones that conflict, and only promote a correction to a general rule after enough agreeing cases, with a person approving it.
  3. Days 9 to 14. Pick up whatever backend work is blocking the team most, with tests first.
